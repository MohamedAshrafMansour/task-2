1-Fake Sender Address (Mismatched Domain)
Sender: administraciones@pentagon-seguridad.cl
Legitimate communications from American Express will always come from official domains like @americanexpress.com or @aexp.com. This email is sent from an unrelated Chilean domain (.cl), likely a compromised or spoofed server.

2-Spelling & Grammatical Mistakes
Subject line: "There's issue with your American Express account" (missing the word "an").
Body text:
-"untill you verify your account" ("until" misspelled with two ls).
-"we advise you to update the information about your account ownership" (awkward, unnatural phrasing).
-"we advise not to notify your account password to anyone" (poor English; legitimate banks typically state "never share your password with anyone").

3-Manufactured Urgency & Coercive Language
Phishing emails often threaten account suspension ("we placed a temporary suspension...") to induce panic and rush you into clicking before verifying legitimacy.


4-Generic Call-to-Action Button
The large blue button ("Click here to review your account now") leads to a fraudulent web page designed to harvest your login credentials and credit card details.
